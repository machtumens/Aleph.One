IThe house

Forty rooms, one kitchen, and the whole bay at the window.

Maison Ardent stands on the old harbour, ten minutes from the ferry pier: the harbourmaster’s house and the warehouse beside it, joined by a courtyard of frangipani and given over, slowly, to rooms.

There is one kitchen and it cooks for the house: breakfast on the terrace as the boats go out, dinner as the lights come on across the water. No conference rooms, no pool bar, no lobby music.

IIIDining

One kitchen, and the morning’s catch.

Two boats land below the terrace at five each morning. The kitchen buys what they bring, cooks it over coconut-husk coals and writes the menu in the afternoon. Guests and the harbour eat in the same room; there is no separate hotel restaurant.

IVJournal

Notes from the harbour wall.

21 September 2026

The spring tides

Twice a year the water climbs over the bottom three steps of the jetty. This week the Tide House guests swam from their doorstep before breakfast, and the kitchen moved dinner one row of tables further up the terrace.

3 September 2026

A grill for the coals

The kitchen has a new grill, built by the boatyard two doors down from an old anchor chain and a sheet of iron. It holds a whole grouper and takes forty minutes to heat. Nobody minds.

12 August 2026

The colour of wet sand

In the quiet weeks we repaint the shutters, one floor at a time. The colour is mixed by eye to match the beach at low tide, which means it is never quite the same twice.
